## Context

See proposal.md - Why. Tree candidates were generated by `vegetation_candidates`
from a per-patch jittered grid phased by patch identity, then thinned to a level
budget. Two consequences: the phase made positions patch-dependent (refining
re-rolled every tree), and the coarse cap of 8 made the far field bare.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Tree positions and acceptance depend only on the world direction.
- Refining a patch keeps every tree already placed.
- The per-patch streaming reservation does not grow, so near-camera LOD selection
  is preserved.

**Non-Goals:**
- Making the far field visually dense. Individual 3D trees cannot reach
  near-field density at coarse levels inside a bounded budget; that needs a true
  impostor representation (a separate change).
- Changing rocks, ground cover, species profiles, or the ecological rules.

## Decisions

- **One shared lattice, nested by construction.** Positions come from global fine
  cells at level 14; a patch selects every `2^(14 - level)`-th cell. A coarse
  patch's selected cells are therefore a subset of the finer patch's, and the
  position depends only on the fine-cell index. Alternatives considered: a
  per-patch grid with world-keyed jitter (positions still differ by level) and a
  world-space constant cell size (near field becomes sparse).
- **Level-independent gate.** `tree_site_gate` uses `height_m`, not
  `mesh_height_m(level)`, so acceptance cannot diverge across LODs. Grounding still
  uses the level-specific mesh height so a tree sits on the rendered surface.
- **Coarse thinning by position rank, fine levels never thin.** A coarse patch
  trims to `COARSE_TREE_BUDGET_CAP` by a deterministic hash of the site position.
  Because finer levels accept every site, the trimmed coarse set remains a subset
  of the finer accepted set; thinning bounds the reservation without reintroducing
  popping.
- **Full side 10, not 12.** The full lattice must stay within the configured tree
  budget (128). A side of 12 gives 144 sites and grew the per-patch reservation
  enough to drop the near-camera LOD one ring in the streaming selection test; 10
  gives 100 sites, keeps the reservation at or below the old value, and still
  leaves a power-of-two coarse divisor (5).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Far field is still sparse] -> Expected: 24 trees over a level-11 patch
  (~1 tree/km²). Documented; genuine density needs impostors.
- [Near-field candidate count falls 128 -> 100] -> Ecology thins most sites
  anyway; the launch-site canopy count is unchanged in the regression test.
- [Thinning breaks nesting] -> Fine levels never thin and the gate is
  level-independent, so the coarse retained set is always a subset of the finer
  accepted set; covered by the domain nesting test and the DEM launch-site test.
- [Reservation accounting drifts] -> `FULL_TREE_BUDGET_CAP` and
  `COARSE_TREE_BUDGET_CAP` are the exact per-patch maxima and feed
  `estimated_patch_bytes`; a const assert keeps the full lattice within the
  configured budget.
